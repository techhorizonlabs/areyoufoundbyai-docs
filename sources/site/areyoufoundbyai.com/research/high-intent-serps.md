# Who wins the moment buyers compare you?

We analysed **1,001 high-buy-intent searches**: "_brand_ vs _rival_", "_brand_ alternatives", pricing, reviews, free trial, across **119 B2B software brands**, and looked at who owns the top-ranked page and what that page is made of. The pattern is stark: **businesses own the searches about buying from them, and have surrendered the searches about choosing them over someone else.**

## Who owns the #1–2 result, by search intent

| Buyer searches… | Queries | Brand's own page wins | Someone else wins |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "_brand_ pricing" | 118 | **81%** | 19% |
| "_brand_ free trial" | 118 | **81%** | 19% |
| "_brand_ cost" | 118 | **81%** | 19% |
| "_brand_ discount" | 118 | 64% | 36% |
| "_brand_ vs _rival_" | 292 | 33% | **67%** |
| "_brand_ review" | 118 | 25% | **75%** |
| "_brand_ alternatives" | 118 | 16% | **84%** |

**Two-thirds of "you vs rival" searches, and five of six "your alternatives" searches, are answered by a page you didn't write.** Often it's the rival's comparison page, an affiliate listicle, or a review aggregator. That page is what a buyer reads at the exact moment they're deciding between you and someone else.

**Why this matters for AI visibility:** when someone asks ChatGPT or Perplexity "should I use _X_ or _Y_?", the assistant's search layer runs exactly these queries, our scans log the searches engines fire while answering, and comparison queries are all over them. The page that wins this SERP is the raw material for AI's answer about you. Surrender the SERP and you've surrendered the answer.

## What the winning pages are actually made of

Feature prevalence across the 941 top-ranked pages we could fully crawl, some of it cuts against popular advice:

| Feature | Share of winners | Read |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title contains the intent word (pricing/vs/alternatives…) | **71%** | table stakes |
| Call-to-action above the fold | **79%** | winners sell |
| Author byline | 77% | common… |
| Author bio | **2%** | …but the "add author bios" advice? Winners skip it |
| FAQ schema | **11%** | not the ranking prerequisite it's sold as |
| Visible "updated" date | 26% | intent-specific (see below) |
| Exact-match H1 | 10% | near-match beats keyword-stuffing |
| Links out to authoritative sources | **~1%** of pages' links | transactional SERPs don't require it |
| Backlinks to the winning page | median **21** | page shape beats raw authority here |

Position 1 vs position 2 is where authority shows: the median #1 page has **82** backlinks to the runner-up's **6**. But a median of 82 is not a moat, it means the podium is reachable with the right page and a modest citation push.

## Each intent has a winning shape

| Intent | The shape that wins |
| --- | --- |
| "vs" (n=274) | head-to-head comparison; 74% use a superlative ("best", "top"), H1 close to the query, honest both-ways structure. FAQ schema on just 7%. |
| "alternatives" (n=109) | the numbered listicle: **79% have a number in the title**, 50% a year, 47% a visible updated date, 93% a byline. The most third-party-owned intent (84%). |
| "pricing" (n=115) | the brand's own plans page: **91% CTA above the fold**, 35% an FAQ block, zero years-in-title, zero listicle theatrics. |
