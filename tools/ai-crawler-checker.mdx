Before any AI engine can cite your business, its crawler must be able to read your site. If GPTBot, ClaudeBot, or PerplexityBot hit a `Disallow` rule in your `robots.txt` (or a firewall block at the CDN level) the engine that depends on that crawler cannot index your content, and you simply won't appear in its answers. The AI Crawler Checker audits your live `robots.txt` against all 15 documented AI user-agents and tells you exactly what each one sees.

<Warning>
  Blocking AI crawlers directly prevents citations. A single misplaced `Disallow` rule can make you invisible to an entire AI engine. Check this before any other GEO fix.
</Warning>

## How to use the checker

Navigate to [areyoufoundbyai.com/tools/ai-crawler-checker](https://areyoufoundbyai.com/tools/ai-crawler-checker), type or paste your domain, and click **Check**. The tool fetches your live `robots.txt` and evaluates every AI crawler rule in seconds.

## The 15 AI crawlers it checks

The checker tests each of the following user-agents against your `robots.txt`:

Every crawler below is one its vendor publicly documents, with the vendor's own page as the source. We do not list a user-agent we cannot point at documentation for.

| Crawler | Who it is | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OAI-SearchBot | OpenAI | ChatGPT search |
| GPTBot | OpenAI | Model training |
| ChatGPT-User | OpenAI | Fetching a page because a user asked |
| Claude-SearchBot | Anthropic | Claude search |
| ClaudeBot | Anthropic | Model training |
| Claude-User | Anthropic | Fetching a page because a user asked |
| PerplexityBot | Perplexity | Search |
| Perplexity-User | Perplexity | Fetching a page because a user asked |
| Googlebot | Google | Google Search |
| Google-Extended | Google | Gemini training and grounding |
| Applebot | Apple | Apple Search, Siri and Spotlight |
| Applebot-Extended | Apple | Foundation model training |
| bingbot | Microsoft | Bing Search, which grounds Copilot |
| meta-externalagent | Meta | Meta AI training and indexing |
| CCBot | Common Crawl | The corpus many models train on |

<Note>
  **Two AI vendors this check cannot assess.** xAI (Grok) and DeepSeek publish no crawler user-agent, and xAI's reported strings are widely reported as not sent, with traffic arriving as ordinary browser agents. With no published identity, there is nothing for a `robots.txt` rule to name, so this checker cannot tell you anything about those two vendors. That is a limit on what `robots.txt` can show you here, not a finding about whether they reach your site by some other route. Their absence from the list is neither permission nor a block, and we would rather say so than leave a gap you might read as access.
</Note>

## What the results show

For each crawler you get one of three statuses:

- **Allowed**, the crawler can access your site normally.
- **Blocked**, an explicit `Disallow` rule prevents access. Fix this immediately.
- **Not Specified**, no rule covers this user-agent. Most crawlers treat this as allowed, but adding an explicit `Allow` rule removes any ambiguity.

The results panel also displays your current `robots.txt` content so you can see exactly which rules are in effect.

## Fix blocked crawlers

If any AI crawler shows as **Blocked**, add explicit allow rules to your `robots.txt`. Place these blocks before any broad `Disallow` rules to ensure they take precedence. The full set to cover all 15 AI crawlers is:

```text
User-agent: GPTBot
Allow: /

User-agent: OAI-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: ClaudeBot
Allow: /

User-agent: PerplexityBot
Allow: /

User-agent: Google-Extended
Allow: /

User-agent: ChatGPT-User
Allow: /

User-agent: Claude-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: Claude-User
Allow: /

User-agent: Perplexity-User
Allow: /

User-agent: Googlebot
Allow: /

User-agent: Applebot
Allow: /

User-agent: Applebot-Extended
Allow: /

User-agent: bingbot
Allow: /

User-agent: meta-externalagent
Allow: /

User-agent: CCBot
Allow: /
```

At minimum, add allow rules for the four engines that drive the most AI-assisted buyer research: ChatGPT (GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot), Perplexity (PerplexityBot), Claude (ClaudeBot), and Google AI Overviews (Google-Extended). You can also disallow expensive dynamic paths (internal search pages, cart endpoints, API routes) while still allowing crawlers on your main content.

<Accordion title="What if my firewall is blocking crawlers despite robots.txt allowing them?">
  A permissive `robots.txt` is a necessary but not sufficient condition. Some WAF (Web Application Firewall) configurations and CDN security rules block user-agents by name or by traffic pattern before `robots.txt` is ever consulted. If the checker shows a crawler as **Allowed** in `robots.txt` but you're still not appearing in that engine's answers, check your Cloudflare, Fastly, or server-level firewall rules for rules that target bot traffic. Verify directly by inspecting your server access logs for the crawler's user-agent string.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Should I allow all crawlers, or can I be selective?">
  You can be selective, blocking a crawler that feeds an AI engine you don't care about is a legitimate choice. However, blocking any of the four major engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Google AI Overviews) makes you completely invisible to that engine's users. Before blocking any AI crawler, consider whether the traffic or compute cost genuinely outweighs the citation benefit.
</Accordion>

<Tip>
  Run the crawler checker after every significant `robots.txt` change and whenever you deploy a new CDN or WAF configuration. A single misplaced rule can silently block all AI indexing.
</Tip>
