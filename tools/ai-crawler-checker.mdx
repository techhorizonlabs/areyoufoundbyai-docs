Before any AI engine can cite your business, its crawler must be able to read your site. If GPTBot, ClaudeBot, or PerplexityBot hit a `Disallow` rule in your `robots.txt` — or a firewall block at the CDN level — the engine that depends on that crawler cannot index your content, and you simply won't appear in its answers. The AI Crawler Checker audits your live `robots.txt` against all 13 major AI user-agents and tells you exactly what each one sees.

<Warning>
  Blocking AI crawlers directly prevents citations. Check this before any other GEO fix.
</Warning>

## How to use the checker

Navigate to [areyoufoundbyai.com/tools/ai-crawler-checker](https://areyoufoundbyai.com/tools/ai-crawler-checker), type or paste your domain, and click **Check**. The tool fetches your live `robots.txt` and evaluates every AI crawler rule in seconds.

## What the results show

For each crawler you get one of three statuses:

- **Allowed** — the crawler can access your site normally.
- **Blocked** — an explicit `Disallow` rule prevents access. Fix this immediately.
- **Not Specified** — no rule covers this user-agent. Most crawlers treat this as allowed, but adding an explicit `Allow` rule removes any ambiguity.

The results panel also displays your current `robots.txt` content so you can see exactly which rules are in effect.

## Fix blocked crawlers

If any AI crawler shows as **Blocked**, add explicit allow rules to the top of your `robots.txt`. The minimum set to cover the four most important AI engines is:

```
User-agent: GPTBot
Allow: /

User-agent: ClaudeBot
Allow: /

User-agent: PerplexityBot
Allow: /

User-agent: Google-Extended
Allow: /
```

Add each blocked crawler from the results table using the same pattern. Place these blocks before any broad `Disallow` rules to ensure they take precedence.

<Accordion title="What if my firewall is blocking crawlers despite robots.txt allowing them?">
  A permissive `robots.txt` is a necessary but not sufficient condition. Some WAF (Web Application Firewall) configurations and CDN security rules block user-agents by name or by traffic pattern before `robots.txt` is ever consulted. If the checker shows a crawler as **Allowed** in `robots.txt` but you're still not appearing in that engine's answers, check your Cloudflare, Fastly, or server-level firewall rules for rules that target bot traffic. Verify directly by inspecting your server access logs for the crawler's user-agent string.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Should I allow all crawlers, or can I be selective?">
  You can be selective — blocking a crawler that feeds an AI engine you don't care about is a legitimate choice. However, the four engines that drive the most AI-assisted buyer research today are ChatGPT (GPTBot), Perplexity (PerplexityBot), Claude (ClaudeBot), and Google AI Overviews (Google-Extended). Block any of those and you're invisible to that engine's users. You can also disallow expensive dynamic paths — internal search pages, cart endpoints, API routes — while still allowing crawlers on your main content.
</Accordion>

<Tip>
  Run the crawler checker after every significant `robots.txt` change and whenever you deploy a new CDN or WAF configuration. A single misplaced rule can silently block all AI indexing.
</Tip>
