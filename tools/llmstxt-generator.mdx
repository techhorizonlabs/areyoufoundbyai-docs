When an AI crawler visits your site, it faces the same problem a new researcher does: which pages are the important ones, and what is this site actually about? `llms.txt` solves this with a single, machine-readable file at your domain root that tells AI assistants exactly which pages matter most, what each one covers, and how your site is structured — all in a format that's cheaper to read than crawling dozens of HTML pages. The llms.txt Generator crawls your sitemap and produces a valid, ready-to-host `llms.txt` file in seconds.

## What llms.txt does

`llms.txt` is a plain-text Markdown file proposed in September 2024 (by Jeremy Howard of fast.ai) as a standard for giving AI systems structured guidance about a website's content. It is analogous to `robots.txt` — which tells crawlers what not to access — but instead tells AI assistants what is most useful to understand about your site.

A well-formed `llms.txt` delivers four benefits:

1. **Faster AI comprehension** — an AI understands your site's purpose and structure from one file rather than dozens of pages.
2. **Controlled narrative** — you choose which pages AI systems see first, shaping how they represent your brand.
3. **Higher citation accuracy** — AI systems that consult `llms.txt` cite the correct, authoritative page for each topic.
4. **Reduced hallucination** — key facts like pricing, services, and locations are stated explicitly.

## The llms.txt format

A valid `llms.txt` file follows this structure:

```markdown
# Your Business Name

> One-line description of your business and what you do.

## Key Pages
- [About](https://yourdomain.com/about): Company overview, founding story, and team
- [Services](https://yourdomain.com/services): Full list of services with pricing overview
- [Contact](https://yourdomain.com/contact): Phone, email, and office locations

## Products
- [Product A](https://yourdomain.com/products/a): Description of what it does and who it's for
- [Product B](https://yourdomain.com/products/b): Description of what it does and who it's for

## Resources
- [Blog](https://yourdomain.com/blog): Articles on industry topics and how-to guides
- [FAQ](https://yourdomain.com/faq): Answers to the most common customer questions

## llms-full.txt
- [Full index](https://yourdomain.com/llms-full.txt)
```

Each entry follows the pattern `- [Page Title](absolute-url): Brief factual description`. Descriptions should state what information is on the page — specific topics, data, or features — not marketing copy.

## Where to host it

Publish your `llms.txt` file at the root of your domain:

```
https://yourdomain.com/llms.txt
```

It must be accessible without redirects and return an HTTP 200 response. Most static-site generators and CMS platforms let you place files in a `public/` or `static/` root folder. For WordPress, place it in `wp-content/uploads/` or use a plugin that serves static files from the root.

<Tip>
  Keep your `llms.txt` under 500 lines. AI crawlers don't follow infinite indexes — a focused, well-curated file outperforms an exhaustive one. Use `llms-full.txt` (linked from your main file) for the complete page inventory.
</Tip>

## The llms-full.txt extended version

`llms-full.txt` is the comprehensive companion file — it lists every indexable page on your site with descriptions. AI systems that want the complete inventory follow the link from your main `llms.txt` to fetch it. This separation keeps the primary file fast to read while making the full index available for deep-crawl scenarios.

<Note>
  Even a minimal `llms.txt` — just the H1 title, a one-line blockquote description, and links to your five most important pages — is meaningfully better than nothing. Start simple and expand it as you refine your content strategy.
</Note>

<Accordion title="How often should I update llms.txt?">
  Update your `llms.txt` whenever you add significant new pages, remove pages that are listed, or change key facts about your business. For active content sites, a monthly review is a good cadence. After updating, submit your `llms.txt` URL via the [IndexNow Submitter](/tools/indexnow) so Bing re-crawls the updated file promptly.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does Google read llms.txt?">
  Google has not officially adopted the `llms.txt` standard, but Google AI Overviews and Gemini crawl sites to inform answers. Google's crawlers can read a plain-text Markdown file at your root. The more immediate beneficiaries are ChatGPT (via its web browsing), Perplexity, and Claude — all of which actively check for `llms.txt` during site research. Maintaining a well-formed file benefits you across all of them.
</Accordion>
