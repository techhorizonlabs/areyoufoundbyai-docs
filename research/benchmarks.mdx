## What Makes a Top-Ranking Business

Across all three countries, the businesses that consistently appear at the top of their category leaderboards share a recognisable pattern. It is not their website technology, their page speed, or their schema implementation. It is their presence across the open web.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Strong YouTube and Reddit presence">
    Top performers have organic, third-party video reviews on YouTube and genuine discussions on Reddit that name them specifically. These are high-trust, highly-indexed signals that AI crawlers surface consistently during retrieval.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Wikipedia or Wikidata entity record">
    The businesses that dominate multiple AI engines in their category almost always have a verifiable entity record on Wikipedia or Wikidata. Even a minimal entry with accurate links establishes the entity anchor that lets AI connect all third-party mentions back to the same business.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Consistent Organization schema">
    Top performers deliver valid Organization and LocalBusiness JSON-LD directly in their server HTML, not via JavaScript injection. The schema includes `sameAs` links pointing to their verified profiles on Google Business, LinkedIn, and industry directories.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Honest comparison pages">
    Many top-ranked businesses have published honest "[them] vs [rival]" pages on their own domain. These pages appear in AI's retrieval layer when buyers ask comparison questions, giving the business control over how it is described at the decision moment.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Published llms.txt">
    A growing number of top performers have published an `llms.txt` file at their domain root. While our research rates this as a minor agent-readiness signal rather than a primary visibility driver, it is a simple step that costs nothing and signals intent to be AI-readable.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
