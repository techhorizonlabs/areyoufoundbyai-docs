Your customers are not all using the same AI assistant. Some open ChatGPT, some ask Google's AI panel, some rely on Perplexity for research, and a fast-growing segment uses Claude, Grok, or DeepSeek. A business that appears on one engine but not the others has fragile visibility — and a business that measures only one engine is flying blind across most of its market. Found by AI measures all seven, majority-votes the results across multiple samples per question, and gives you a per-engine breakdown so you can see exactly where you're winning and where you're absent.

## The Seven Engines

| Engine | Behaviour & sourcing notes |
|--------|---------------------------|
| **ChatGPT** (OpenAI) | Uses web search fan-out before answering buyer questions. Sources local business citations heavily from directories and Bing. Strong entity-recognition from Wikidata and knowledge graph signals. GPTBot is the crawler to allow. |
| **Perplexity** | Retrieval-first — runs live web searches before every answer. Highly sensitive to fresh indexed content and citation depth. Businesses with strong directory and press footprints appear here earliest. PerplexityBot is the crawler to allow. |
| **Gemini** (Google) | Draws on Google's own index, Google Business Profile data, and the Knowledge Graph. For local businesses, GBP completeness has a direct effect. Google-Extended is the crawler to allow. |
| **Claude** (Anthropic) | Balances model knowledge with retrieval. Favours well-attributed, credentialled content and E-E-A-T signals. Less locally anchored than Google but strong on expertise indicators. ClaudeBot is the crawler to allow. |
| **Grok** (xAI) | Real-time access to X (Twitter) signals and current web content. Responsive to brand conversations happening on social platforms. Freshness of mentions carries extra weight. |
| **DeepSeek** | Strong on document-dense, factual content. Favours structured, citable passages from authoritative domains. Growing rapidly in Asian markets with global English reach. |
| **Google AI Overviews** | Appears above organic results on roughly one in four searches; absorbs clicks that previously reached websites. Driven by Google's AI Mode and fan-out query technique — issues multiple related searches before synthesising an answer. Being cited inside it is the new position one. |

## Why Engines Disagree

AI engines are not a single oracle. The same buyer question asked across all seven on the same day can produce seven different shortlists — and any individual engine asked twice in a row can produce two different answers. Three factors drive disagreement:

**Majority-voted sampling.** Found by AI asks each question up to three times per engine and takes the majority result. A business named in 2 of 3 samples is "sometimes named," not simply "named." A single result is a coin-flip near the margin, not ground truth. This sampling discipline is the only honest way to report AI visibility — a single snapshot proves nothing.

**Source differences.** Each engine retrieves from a different combination of its own index, the live web, platform-specific data (GBP for Google, Bing-indexed directories for ChatGPT, X for Grok), and its base training. A business well-represented on Google's directory graph may appear on Gemini but not Perplexity. A business with fresh press coverage may appear on Perplexity before it appears elsewhere.

**Freshness windows.** Retrieval-first engines like Perplexity and ChatGPT (in search mode) can index and surface new mentions within days. Base model knowledge in engines without live retrieval updates on training cycles — typically months. This means a new directory listing or press mention can shift your score on some engines weeks before others.

## What Counts as Being Named

Found by AI scores a business as **named** when its exact business name appears verbatim in the engine's answer to a buyer question. A citation link without the name in the answer text does not count. A near-match or partial name does not count.

This precision matters. Engines sometimes reference a domain in their source list without naming the business in prose — that is a citation, not a recommendation. The distinction reflects what a buyer actually experiences: they read the answer, not the footnotes.

Every verbatim answer is stored in your report exactly as the engine gave it, with the model, date, sample index, and fan-out search queries the engine ran before answering.
