Running your first scan takes about 60 seconds and gives you two scores that show where you stand with AI engines right now: an **AI Visibility Score** that measures whether AI assistants are actually naming your business when buyers ask, and an **AI Readiness Score** that measures how well your site is built to earn those recommendations. This page walks you through the entire process from entering your URL to understanding what every section of your first report means.

<Steps>

  <Step title="Navigate to areyoufoundbyai.com">
    Open [areyoufoundbyai.com](https://areyoufoundbyai.com) in your browser. You do not need an account to run a free scan, just land on the homepage and you are ready to start.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Enter your website URL">
    Type or paste your website URL into the scan field in the centre of the page and press **Scan**. Use your full domain (e.g. `https://example.com`). The scanner uses the URL to identify your business and pull together the signals it will check.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Wait for the scan to complete (~60 seconds)">
    The scanner queries the AI engines live. It does not use cached data. Because it is asking real engines real questions in real time, the scan takes roughly 60 seconds. A progress indicator shows you what is happening. Do not close the tab while the scan is running.
  </Step>

  <Step title="View your Visibility and Readiness scores">
    When the scan finishes, your report opens automatically. Two scores appear at the top: your **AI Visibility Score** (0–100) and your **AI Readiness Score** (0–100). These are the two numbers that matter most. One tells you where you stand today, the other tells you how much capacity your site has to improve.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Expand verbatim answers for each engine">
    Scroll down to the **Engine answers** section. Each AI engine has an expandable card showing the verbatim text it returned when asked a buyer question. Open each one to read exactly what the engine said, and whether it named you, named a competitor, or gave a generic answer.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Review competitor names and sources">
    The **Competitors** section lists every business the engines named instead of you during this scan. The **Sources** section shows which pages on the web the engines cited in their answers. Together these two sections tell you who is winning your category right now, and where they are getting their authority from.
  </Step>

</Steps>

## Understanding your first report

### AI Visibility Score

Your AI Visibility Score reflects how often the engines named your business across the buyer questions asked during this scan. A score above **70** means you are regularly present in AI answers. A score between **40 and 70** means you appear sometimes but inconsistently. Below **40** means the engines are largely recommending others when your buyers ask.

Visibility is primarily driven by off-page signals, independent mentions, third-party citations, and brand authority signals that live outside your own website. A single scan gives you a snapshot; monitoring over time shows you whether your efforts are moving the needle.

### AI Readiness Score

Your AI Readiness Score is a composite of six dimensions that measure how well your site is built to earn AI recommendations. Unlike Visibility, Readiness is largely within your direct control. It reflects your content structure, technical setup, schema markup, and how well you signal authority. The dimensions and their weights are:

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---|
| AI Citability | 25% |
| Brand Authority | 20% |
| Content E-E-A-T | 20% |
| Technical GEO | 15% |
| Schema & Structured Data | 10% |
| Platform Optimization | 10% |

### What the competitors section shows

The competitors section lists the businesses that AI engines recommended in your place during this scan. Each name represents a real answer an engine gave to a real buyer question. This is your page list for comparison and "alternatives" content, each competitor is a "you vs them" opportunity worth owning.

### What the sources section shows

The sources section lists the third-party pages the engines cited when forming their answers. These are the domains that have authority in your category right now. If you appear on few or none of them, earning a presence there (through reviews, listings, and editorial mentions) is one of the highest-leverage things you can do to improve your Visibility Score.
